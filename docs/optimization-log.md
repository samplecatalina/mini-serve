# Optimization log

Each entry records what was expected before a change was measured, what was measured, and why the two differ. Performance numbers link to rows under `results/` or reports under `profiling/`; correctness findings link to tests.

## M1 — Continuous batching, paged KV cache, sampling, API

M1 has no performance measurements: its changes are judged by the correctness anchor and by stress tests, not by throughput. The tolerance policy used from here on: a batched or paged path may diverge from the reference only at a position where the reference's top-2 logit gap is at most ε = 0.5, and, fed its own output, the reference must rank each chosen token within ε of its maximum ("teacher-forced margin").

Concurrent: 5/6 (all at once) and 6/6 (staggered) sequences diverge within 128 tokens; at every divergence the reference top-2 gap is 0.0 or 0.125; teacher-forced margin at most 0.1875. ## M2 — Prefix cache and chunked prefill

Measured on an RTX 4060 Laptop GPU with the engine-level harness (`bench/offline.py`: open-loop arrivals straight into the engine, settings alternated in one process, median of 3 runs per setting). Each prediction below was committed before the measurement it predicts. The share-of-peak figure is left out until this repository measures its own peak bandwidth and GEMM throughput.

## M4 — Primary benchmark on the L40S, ablations, head-to-head

Measured on a single NVIDIA L40S (Ada, 48 GB) on a Bridges-2 GPU node, with
[genai-bench](https://github.com/sgl-project/genai-bench) as the load generator from a separate container
image, over the OpenAI-compatible HTTP endpoint. Workload `D(1024,256)`: 1024-token prompts sampled from a
corpus, 256 output tokens, `temperature=0`, `ignore_eos=true`. Each concurrency level is a separate run; the
first 30 s of every run is discarded, because the clock needs about that long to settle. Every run writes a
sidecar with a 15-item fairness checklist, the clock and power trace, and the counters the engine itself kept
(the batch it actually formed, mixed-step share, preemptions), because the number of concurrent requests is
not the batch the engine forms and a load generator cannot see the latter. Each prediction below was committed
before the measurement it predicts.

The denominator is this repository's own measurement of device-to-device copy bandwidth (`bench/roofline.py`,
largest buffer size, since a decode step streams 1.19 GB of weights and no L2 can hold that): **654.5 GB/s** on
the L40S and **205.3 GB/s** on the RTX 4060 Laptop GPU. A decode step over a batch `B` at context `C` moves
`1.192 GB + B·C·112 KiB`, which turns any measured step time into a share of that floor — a figure that does
not depend on the device, the batch or the context, and so can be read across both machines.
